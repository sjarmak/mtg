# The interaction lever that is not flash

Measured 2026-08-21 (`mtg-8sm6`), against this worktree at `55d2c532`. Every count below came from
a run over the committed set fixture, the committed fixture corpora and the shipped evaluator on
this machine; nothing is estimated and nothing is carried over from another report. Where a number
in the bead disagreed with the tree, the tree won and §2.1 says so.

**Verdict (argued in §5): the cheapest real interaction lever already ships and has never been
printed. `TargetFilter.combat` lets a card say "target attacking creature" or "target blocking
creature"; it is implemented in the DSL, answered by the kernel against live combat state, printed
correctly by the oracle renderer, cast at the right moment by the simulator's existing hold policy,
and it appears on zero of the 368 cards in the flagship fixture. Printing it on hand-authored
instants costs zero new vocabulary, zero schema movement, zero fixture re-records and zero engine
work. The measured price of the runner-up — putting the same field in front of the generator — is
163 of 172 recorded fixtures re-recorded to unlock two common slots out of 81, which is a worse
trade than the one flash was refused for.**

This bead measured; it printed nothing. What follows is the census that says what is actually
missing, a price list for each way of fixing it, the ranking those two produce, and one
recommendation with three cards.

**Answered 2026-08-21 by `mtg-85d4`.** The three cards were printed. `authoredCards` went from 121
to 124 and the fixture from 368 cards to 371: `Light Arrow` ({1}{W} instant, exile target attacking
or blocking creature with power 3 or greater), `Bomb Flower` ({R} instant, 3 damage to target
attacking or blocking creature with power 4 or less) and `Merfolk's Undertow` ({U} instant, return
target attacking creature to its owner's hand). The recommendation in §5 said destroy for the white
one; the set's own `colorSignatures` gate refused that, because `destroyPermanent` is on white's
absent list in this set, so it exiles instead. Every count below is the reading taken before those
three existed and is left at that reading on purpose: it is the evidence for the decision, not a
description of the set today.

---

## 1. What the set is actually short of

Round-one feedback said three things: too many on-death and on-attack triggers, removal that is
hyper-powerful, and a shortage of spells that are not destroy / +N+N / bounce / tap. Two of those
are claims about distribution and can be checked. The census below ran over
the committed flagship set fixture under `packages/setgen/fixtures/sets/`, which holds 368 cards: 247
generated by the paid run and its successors, plus 121 hand-authored ones appended by
`packages/setgen/src/authored.ts`.

By card kind: 208 creatures, 87 instants, 34 sorceries, 20 artifacts, 16 enchantments, 3
planeswalkers.

**Triggers.** 150 triggered abilities across 144 cards. The distribution confirms the complaint
almost exactly: `selfEnters` 47, `selfDies` 30, `selfAttacks` 17,
`selfBlocksOrIsBlockedByGreaterPower` 12, `anotherControlledCreatureEnters` 11,
`selfDealsCombatDamageToCreature` 8, `youCastInstantOrSorcery` 6, `beginningOfYourEndStep` 5,
`youGainLife` 3, `youCastSpell` 3, `controlledCreatureAttacksAlone` 2,
`selfDealsCombatDamageToPlayer` 2, `selfBlocks` 2, `selfDiesNotSacrificed` 1,
`beginningOfYourUpkeep` 1. Ninety-four of the 150 fire on the card's own arrival, attack or death.

The load-bearing half of that count is what is missing. `TRIGGER_CONDITIONS`
(`packages/dsl/src/vocabulary.ts:1057`) has 24 members, and seven of them watch a table rather than
a body: the five `aPlayerCasts<Color>Spell` conditions, `opponentDealtNoncombatDamage`, and
`beginningOfEndStep`. **All seven are printed zero times.** The vocabulary for opponent-facing
triggers is already in the tree and no card uses it.

**Activated abilities.** 39 of 368 cards carry one, which is 10.6%: 21 creatures, 13 artifacts, 3
planeswalkers, 2 enchantments; by rarity 8 common, 18 uncommon, 10 rare, 3 mythic; by provenance 23
hand-authored and 22 generated (six cards carry more than one). Ten of the 39 are mana abilities
and do nothing to a board. Only two name an unambiguously opposing target, and only 13 name a
target that could belong to an opponent at all. So the interaction budget notionally available at
instant speed from permanents already in play is being spent almost entirely on the controller's
own side.

**Statics.** 43 static abilities: `statBonus` 26, `grantKeyword` 11, `mustBeBlockedIfAble` 3,
`attacksEachCombatIfAble` 2, `statBonusPer` 1. Those are static abilities and only static abilities,
which is the scope this paragraph is about; they are not the set's whole count of any modification
named in them. A continuous clause is printed in three places, and `packages/setgen/tools/pool-census.ts`
reads all three: 26 cards carry a `statBonus` static ability, six more carry the same modification
in an equip clause and two on an Aura, so the set's `statBonus` card count is 34. Quoting the 26 as
the set's `statBonus` total is what a later census did, and it cost that census eight cards. Every one is scoped `self` (13),
`creaturesYouControl` (13) or `otherCreaturesYouControl` (17), because `STATIC_SCOPES`
(`vocabulary.ts:792`) has exactly those three members and the comment above it says that scopes
reaching an opponent's permanents are deliberately outside DSL v1. No static in the set touches the
other player. **`costReduction` appears on zero of 368 cards.**

**Target narrowing.** This is the finding the rest of the document turns on. Across 368 cards there
are **11 narrowed targets**: restriction `withCounter` 4, `tapped` 3, `maxPower` 1 (spread over
seven cards), and filter `cardTypes` 3. **Combat-role filters: zero.** Of the 87 instants in the
set, five narrow their target by anything at all. So the complaint that removal is hyper-powerful
is, mechanically, the observation that essentially every removal spell in the set is unconditional,
and the set is unconditional not because the DSL insists on it but because nobody has printed the
conditional form.

## 2. The price list

Every candidate below is priced against the same four questions the bead asks. Three of them reduce
to one number, so it is worth stating the number once.

### 2.1 Which schema moves, and what that costs

`packages/dsl/src/effects.ts` declares three unions and the distinction between them is the whole
cost model. `EffectSchema` (line 1706) is the engine contract. `CardEffectSchema` (line 1735) is
what a card's own effect list may print: the engine vocabulary plus a long tail of unpriced
primitives that exist to be hand-authored. `ModelEffectSchema` (line 1773) is what the generator may
answer with, and it is a strict subset built over `ModelTargetSpecSchema`, which is
`z.object({ kind: ModelTargetKindSchema })` and carries no `filter` and no `restriction`
(`packages/dsl/src/targets.ts:317`).

The fixture key is `sha256` over `{v, system, prompt, schema}` (`packages/llm/src/schema.ts:68`), so
a field added to a schema the model is shown re-addresses every recorded fixture whose answer schema
embeds it, and a paid live run is the only way back. Re-measured against the corpora in this
worktree:

| Change                                                                | Fixtures re-keyed | Of  |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | --- |
| A hand-authored card using existing vocabulary                        | 0                 | 172 |
| A new `TRIGGER_CONDITIONS` member kept off `MODEL_TRIGGER_CONDITIONS` | 0                 | 172 |
| A new member added to `MODEL_TRIGGER_CONDITIONS`                      | 76                | 172 |
| `filter` or `restriction` added to `ModelTargetSpecSchema`            | 163               | 172 |
| A per-batch answer-schema tier holding the two removal notes          | 26                | 172 |

The bead quotes the flash lane's figure of 143 of 151. That figure is correct for
`packages/setgen/fixtures/llm/` taken alone, and it reproduces exactly: 143 of those 151 fixtures
embed the effect union or a target kind. It understates the corpus, because
`packages/setgen/fixtures/llm-hearthglass/` holds 21 more recorded fixtures and 20 of them embed it
too. **The real blast radius of a `ModelEffectSchema` widening is 163 of 172, not 143 of 151.** The
trigger-condition figure is 67 of 151 in the main corpus and 76 of 172 overall, and 132 of 172
prompts print the `<effect_vocabulary>` block verbatim.

The last row is the escape hatch and it deserves its own sentence. Per-batch answer-schema tiering
(argued in `packages/setgen/src/filled.ts`'s header, worked through for
`ZoneReachingModelEffectSchema`) makes a widening into a second union shown only to batches whose
slots ask for it, so batches asking for none replay for free. Applied to conditional targeting, the
batches that would move are the ones holding the two role notes that describe the gap: 14 fixtures
pin the `removalCombatConditional` note and 12 pin the `removalSmallConditional` note, with no
overlap, so 26 of 172. That is the cheap version of the expensive candidate, and §3.6 measures what
26 fixtures buy.

### 2.2 Whether the evaluator can price it

A lever the deck evaluator scores at zero cannot pass the balance gate, which is the failure already
recorded as `mtg-gloz` for `card.keywordAbilities`. Every candidate below was priced by actually
constructing the card, parsing it with `safeParseCard` and running
`evaluateCard(card, DEFAULT_SCORE_WEIGHTS).score`. Scores are the shipped weights, not a model.

Baselines first, so the numbers mean something. A vanilla 2/3 for `{2}{W}` scores **-0.350**. A
three-mana vanilla artifact scores **-2.650**. An unconditional `{1}{W}` "destroy target creature"
scores **3.000**, and an unconditional `{R}` "deals 3 damage to any target" also scores **3.000**.

## 3. The candidates

### 3.1 Conditional targeting that reads the opponent's board (recommended)

**What exists today.** `TargetFilterSchema` carries an optional `combat` field
(`packages/dsl/src/targets.ts:276`) over `TARGET_COMBAT_ROLES = ['attacking', 'blocking',
'attackingOrBlocking']` (`vocabulary.ts:744`). `TargetRestrictionSchema` (`targets.ts:154`) carries
seven members, of which `maxPower`, `minPower`, `tapped` and `untapped` all read the opponent's
board. `TargetSpecSchema` (`targets.ts:289`) admits both fields; `ModelTargetSpecSchema`
(`targets.ts:317`) admits neither.

The kernel answers the combat half against live state:
`packages/kernel/src/target-filter.ts:67` reads `state.combat.attacks` and line 72 reads
`state.combat.blocks`, and the docblock at line 19 argues why it has to be there rather than in the
characteristic check (CR 506.4 makes "attacking" a property of the combat, not of the object). The
oracle renderer prints it in English without any work: a probe card rendered as
`Destroy target attacking or blocking creature with power 3 or greater.` from the DSL alone.

**What would have to be added.** Nothing, on the hand-authored route. Not `EffectSchema`, not
`CardEffectSchema`, not `ModelEffectSchema`, not `EFFECT_PRICING`, not a kernel arm, not a print
template. The one thing that would be nice to have and is not required is a Forge export mapping,
and the field predates this lane so that question is already settled wherever it needed to be.

The simulator needs no change either, and this is the single sharpest difference from flash. The
flash refusal included a rewrite of the cast policy because a flash creature has no window in the
bot's model. A conditional instant needs no new window: `castTimingAllows`
(`packages/sim/src/policies/cast.ts:47`) already returns true on the opponent's turn, and
`COMBAT_RESPONSE_STEPS` (line 32) already admits `declareAttackers`, `declareBlockers`,
`firstStrikeDamage` and `combatDamage` under the default `holdInstants: true`. The bot is already
holding instants for exactly the moment these cards want to be cast; it simply has none to hold.

**How many cards it yields.** On the hand-authored route the skeleton does not bound it, because
hand-authored cards are appended after generation rather than filling slots. The bound is
`authoredCards: z.array(CardSchema).max(121)` (`packages/setgen/src/brief.ts:612`), and it is full
at 121 of 121, so the first printing of this family requires raising that cap — which the docblock
says must be typed and argued, and §6 files it. Every one of the 87 instants in the set is a
candidate for a conditional sibling, and the five common instant slots that most obviously want one
are `removalCombatConditional` (W), `removalSmallConditional` (B), `removalOvercosted` (B),
`burnTwo` (R) and the three `combatTrick` slots (W, B, R).

**Whether the evaluator can price it.** It can, and it prices it wrong in an interesting direction.
`targetRestrictionFactor` (`packages/deckbuild/src/evaluate.ts:1373`) reads
`effect.target.restriction` and applies `restrictedTargetFactor` (0.5) when one is present. **It
never reads `effect.target.filter`.** So the measured result is:

| Probe                                                                                 | Score  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| `{1}{W}` instant, destroy target creature                                             | 3.000  |
| `{1}{W}` instant, destroy target attacking creature                                   | 3.000  |
| `{1}{W}` instant, destroy target attacking or blocking creature                       | 3.000  |
| `{R}` instant, 3 damage to any target                                                 | 3.000  |
| `{R}` instant, 3 damage to target attacking or blocking creature                      | 3.000  |
| `{1}{B}` instant, destroy target untapped creature                                    | 1.700  |
| `{1}{B}` instant, destroy target attacking creature with power 3 or greater           | 1.700  |
| `{R}` instant, 3 damage to target attacking or blocking creature with power 4 or less | 1.975  |
| `{1}{U}` instant, return target attacking creature to hand                            | -0.100 |
| `{U}` instant, return target attacking creature to hand                               | 0.450  |
| `{1}{B}` instant, target attacking creature gets -3/-3                                | 1.140  |
| `{1}{B}` instant, target attacking creature gets -3/-3, gain 2 life                   | 1.340  |
| `{W}` instant, tap target attacking creature                                          | 0.050  |

This is `mtg-gloz`'s failure mode pointed the other way: the field is not invisible enough to zero
the card, it is invisible enough to make a strictly weaker card score identically to a strictly
stronger one. That is a real bug and §6 files it. **It has since been fixed** (`mtg-xiis`): the
discount reaches either narrowing field and the seven scores that moved are listed in §4.2, so the
table above is the reading that argued the case rather than the prices today. It is also, for this lane, a bug with a workaround
that is better design anyway: **pairing the combat filter with a restriction gets the card priced
correctly today**, because the restriction is read. `destroy target attacking creature with power 3
or greater` lands at 1.700, which is a discount of 43% against the unconditional 3.000 and is still
comfortably draftable. So the lever survives the fix, and the family can be printed on the shape
that already prices honestly while the fix is pending.

### 3.2 Activated abilities on permanents already in play

**What exists today.** Everything; 39 cards already carry one. A permanent already on the
battlefield is instant-speed by construction and needs no timing vocabulary.

**What would have to be added.** Nothing.

**How many cards it yields.** Unbounded by vocabulary, bounded by price.

**Whether the evaluator can price it.** It prices activations, but thinly, and the numbers are
measured rather than guessed, which makes them hard to argue with.
`packages/deckbuild/src/config.ts` carries `activationUseCount: 0.608` (measured, n=32,446) and
`activationTapFactor: 0.397` (measured, n=2,199), so a tap-cost activated ability is expected to be
used 0.241 times per arrival of the permanent. Against `activationCostPerMana: 0.35` that is a thin
margin, and it shows: a 2/3 for `{2}{W}` with `{1}{W}, {T}: tap target attacking creature` scored
**-0.350**, identical to the vanilla 2/3 for `{2}{W}`. The ability contributed exactly nothing to
the card's score.

This is not an evaluator bug; it is the evaluator reporting that a once-per-two-arrivals tap-down is
worth about as much as nothing, which is defensible. It means the candidate is real but its ceiling
is set by how much value can be crammed into a single activation, and the cards that clear it are
uncommon-and-up. It cannot carry a common-slot interaction program.

### 3.3 Opponent-action triggers that already exist and have never been printed

**What exists today.** Seven `TRIGGER_CONDITIONS` members watch the table rather than the source's
own body: `aPlayerCastsWhiteSpell`, `...Blue`, `...Black`, `...Red`, `...Green`,
`opponentDealtNoncombatDamage`, `beginningOfEndStep`. All seven are hand-authored only, deliberately
absent from `MODEL_TRIGGER_CONDITIONS`, which is frozen at `['selfEnters', 'selfAttacks',
'selfDies']` (`vocabulary.ts:1412`). All seven have kernel arms and print templates already.

**What would have to be added.** Nothing to print the seven. A genuinely new one — "whenever an
opponent attacks", "whenever an opponent draws a card" — owes five sites: a
`TRIGGER_PRINT_TEMPLATES` entry (`vocabulary.ts:1422`), a `FORGE_TRIGGER_MODES` entry
(`packages/forge-export/src/vocabulary-map.ts:480`), a `DEFAULT_TRIGGER_FIRE_COUNT` row
(`deckbuild/config.ts:604`), a `DEFAULT_TRIGGER_VALUE` row (`sim/config.ts:403`), and a
`conditionsFrom` arm in `packages/kernel/src/triggers.ts`. Zero fixtures, as long as it stays off
`MODEL_TRIGGER_CONDITIONS`; 76 of 172 if it does not.

**How many cards it yields.** Unbounded by vocabulary on the hand-authored route, and zero on the
generator route without paying the 76.

**Whether the evaluator can price it.** Barely, and against numbers nobody measured. The
`DEFAULT_TRIGGER_FIRE_COUNT` table distinguishes measured rows from guessed ones and says which is
which: `selfEnters: 1.0`, `selfAttacks: 1.221`, `selfDies: 0.463`, `beginningOfYourEndStep: 2.708`
and `youCastSpell: 1.662` are measured over the sweep, but every one of the seven opponent-facing
conditions carries a guess — `aPlayerCasts<Color>Spell` at 1.6 across all five,
`opponentDealtNoncombatDamage` at 0.15, and `beginningOfEndStep` at 5.416 derived as twice the
your-end-step figure rather than observed. Probes land where those guesses put them: a 1/3 for
`{1}{U}` with `whenever a player casts a blue spell, scry 1` scored **0.500**, a `{2}{R}` 1/3 flier
with an `opponentDealtNoncombatDamage` ping scored **-0.008**, and a `{2}{B}` enchantment with
`beginningOfEndStep: each opponent loses 1 life` scored **-1.108**. For comparison a 2/3 for
`{2}{W}` with a `selfBlocks` lifegain trigger scored -0.274 and a 3/3 for `{2}{G}` with a
`selfBlocks` pump scored 1.013.

So this candidate is cheap to print and expensive to trust: the cards it makes are priced against
five unmeasured constants, and a balance verdict computed from a guess is a guess. It ranks second
and it should be printed after the fire counts are measured, not before.

### 3.4 Instants made to care about the opponent's state

This is §3.1 with a different emphasis and it is not a separate lever: the only vocabulary an
instant has for caring about the opponent's state is the target filter and the target restriction.
Measured separately because the bead lists it separately, and the measurement is that its distinctive
half — payoff riders conditioned on the opponent — has no vocabulary at all. There is no
`countMatching` over an opponent's board, no conditional effect node, and no static scope that reads
one. `{1}{U}` "return target attacking creature to its owner's hand, then scry 1" scored **-0.100**,
which is the honest verdict on the shape: the rider is free (`scry` prices at base 0.0 and perUnit
0.0) and the bounce is flat, so two mana buys a card nobody drafts. Folded into §3.1.

### 3.5 Cost-increase and tax statics (rejected)

**What exists today.** Cost modification exists in one direction only. `CostReductionSchema`
(`packages/dsl/src/cost-reduction.ts`) is `{ amount: z.int().positive(), cardType: ... }`, a
card-level field rather than a `StaticModification`, and `describeCostReduction` hardcodes the
string `"…you cast cost {N} less to cast."` (line 49). `effectiveManaCost`
(`packages/kernel/src/cost.ts:130`) skips every modifier whose `controller !== player` (line 139),
so the kernel has no path by which one player's permanent changes another player's costs.
`STATIC_SCOPES` has three members and none of them reaches an opponent.

**What would have to be added.** A sign or a direction on `CostReductionSchema` (the schema refuses
a negative amount outright: a probe with `amount: -1` was rejected `SCHEMA_INVALID`, "expected number
to be >0"), a rewrite of the print function, an inversion of the controller check in
`effectiveManaCost`, a fourth `STATIC_SCOPES` member (a probe with scope `creaturesOpponentsControl`
was rejected `SCHEMA_INVALID`), and the whole of the pricing that does not exist yet.

**Whether the evaluator can price it.** No, and this is a second confirmed instance of `mtg-gloz`
rather than a risk of one. The string `costReduction` appears nowhere in `packages/deckbuild` or
`packages/sim`. Measured: a three-mana vanilla artifact scores -2.650, and the same artifact
carrying `costReduction: { amount: 1, cardType: 'creature' }` scores **-2.650**. Identical to three
decimal places. The field is worth exactly zero to the deck builder today, on the reduction side
that already ships.

**Rejected.** Five sites of new work across three packages, a schema that refuses the sign, a kernel
that refuses the direction, and a pricing hole that has to be fixed before the first card can be
evaluated at all — to print a family the set currently has zero appetite for, since it has printed
zero cost reducers either. This is the most expensive candidate on the board and it is more expensive
than flash.

### 3.6 Conditional targeting shown to the generator (rejected)

Worth pricing separately because it is the version of the recommendation that scales without hand
authoring, and because refusing it is what makes the recommendation honest.

**What would have to be added.** `filter` or `restriction` on `ModelTargetSpecSchema`, which is the
`ModelEffectSchema` widening in everything but name, because `ModelEffectSchema` is built over
`ModelTargetSpecSchema`. Cost: **163 of 172 recorded fixtures re-recorded**, or 26 of 172 under a
per-batch tier confined to the two role slots whose notes describe this exact gap.

**How many cards it yields, by the published skeleton.** Counted, not guessed. The play-booster
profile in `packages/design-data/data/skeleton-play-booster-2024.json` sets `{common: 81, uncommon:
100, rare: 60, mythic: 20, total: 261}`. Its common spell slots number **31** across the five colors
plus colorless (W 4, U 7, B 6, R 6, G 5, C 3), of which **17 are instants** and **15 carry a
substitution note** saying the DSL cannot express the slot's intent. Exactly two of those notes
describe this gap: `removalCombatConditional` (W common instant, "DSL v0 has no attack/block
conditions; the slot prints unconditional removal or a tap") and `removalSmallConditional` (B common
instant, "DSL v0 has no power/toughness conditions on removal; the slot prints it unconditional").

Both sentences are already false about the DSL and true only about the model's schema, which is what
`packages/setgen/test/substitution-notes.test.ts` records: its `NOT_AN_EFFECT_PRIMITIVE` ledger
corrects the first to "`TargetFilter.combat` exists and is hand-authored only; `ModelTargetSpec` is
`{ kind }` and carries no filter" and the second to "`TargetRestriction.maxPower` exists;
`ModelTargetSpec` is `{ kind }` and carries none". That test's stated rule is that a false note is
tolerated exactly while a recorded prompt holds it, and 26 recorded prompts hold these two.

**So the trade is 26 fixtures for 2 common slots per 261-card build, or 163 fixtures for the same 2
slots without the tiering device.** Flash was refused at 143 fixtures for roughly 1 card. Two cards
for 26 is a better ratio than that and still a bad deal, because the same two cards can be
hand-authored for nothing, and because a generator that can say "target attacking creature" will say
it in slots that did not ask for it. **Rejected as a first move**, and reconsidered only after the
hand-authored family has shipped and a sweep has shown the format wants more of it than 121 authored
slots can hold.

Rows 1 through 5 all cost zero fixtures, which is the finding: the interaction requirement does not
need a paid re-record at all. The reason it looked like it did is that the three mechanisms that would
express it — the target filter, the seven table-watching trigger conditions and the toll clause — are
all hand-authored-only, and hand-authored-only reads as "not available" if you only ever look at what
the generator can emit.

### 4.1 The toll clause, added 2026-08-21 (`mtg-g39v`)

The first pass at this table missed CR 118.8 outright, and the miss is the same one the paragraph
above describes: `Card.unless` is hand-authored-only, so a reader looking at what the generator can
emit sees nothing there. `packages/dsl/src/unless.ts` carries `{ payer, cost }` and every downstream
seam already answers it — the kernel pauses the resolution (`unless-choice.ts`, and `legal.ts` offers
the two answers as an `answerUnless` pair), the greedy bot weighs the price against what the spell
would do and the random bot samples it, `answerUnless` in the simulator's trigger policy converts the
toll through `tollManaWeight`, the play surface prompts for it, the referee frame cites CR 118.8 to
the model, the Forge exporter emits `UnlessCost` / `UnlessPayer`, and the oracle renderer prints
`Destroy target creature unless its controller pays {2}.` from the DSL alone. Measured here rather
than taken on trust: a probe card carrying the clause parses, prints and scores.

**Why it earns row 2 rather than row 1.** It is the only lever on this board that changes what the
_opponent_ does about a card rather than what the card is allowed to point at. Row 1 makes removal
miss; a toll makes removal cost the other player something even when it hits, and a player who cannot
pay is not asked at all (`unless-choice.ts` resolves the spell as though the clause were not printed),
so it is removal that stays removal against a tapped-out board. That is the half of "removal is
hyper-powerful" that a narrowed target does not reach.

**Priced, and priced honestly today.** `tolledSpellFactor: 0.8` in `packages/deckbuild/src/config.ts`
shipped 2026-08-21 at `8b7baddb`, so the builder plays a tolled spell below the unconditional one
rather than at parity: `{1}{W}` "destroy target creature" scores 3.000 and the same card with a {2}
toll scores **2.480**, a 17% discount against the 43% a narrowed target takes. The seam deliberately
does not read the toll's own _size_ — {2} and {4} both score 2.480 — because `evaluateCard` takes a
card and not a board, and what makes a price bite is how much mana the payer has open. The simulator
is the seam that can see that, and it does.

**The bounds the clause carries**, stated in `packages/dsl/src/validate/effects.ts` and covered by
`packages/dsl/test/unless.test.ts`. The clause is refused on a permanent, refused alongside `modes`,
refused alongside `may`, and refused on a spell printing more than one effect. On top of those,
`UNLESS_PAYER_TARGETS` reads the payer off what the spell targets, so `targetController` admits
`targetCreature` and `targetArtifactOrEnchantment` only and `targetPlayer` admits `targetPlayer` and
`targetOpponent` only. That last one is the place the shape is narrower than it reads, and it is
measured here rather than inferred: probes putting a toll on a counterspell and on "deals 3 damage to
any target" were both refused `UNLESS_PAYER_HAS_NO_TARGET`. So the toll goes on removal and on a
player-facing spell, and not on a counter.

**Sites: one, not zero, and this is the row's only cost.** No committed set prints the shape — zero
occurrences across all three set fixtures under `packages/setgen/fixtures/sets/` — and `unless` is one
of the seven `CARD_SHAPES` members (`packages/dsl/src/card-shape.ts`) that the shape-coverage ledger
names as printed nowhere. That ledger fails in both directions: it refuses a claim for a shape nothing
prints, and it refuses a printed shape no gate opens. So the first card carrying a toll turns it red
until a claim naming the gate that has the shape in front of it is added beside it. Working as
designed, and worth stating in the row rather than discovering in a run.

**Row 7 is the generator half and it is `mtg-7smq`'s open decision.** The clause is absent from the
model card schema entirely: the string appears in none of the 172 recorded fixture schemas and nowhere
in `packages/setgen/src`. Adding it means a field on `spellShape` (`packages/setgen/src/filled.ts`),
which 163 of the 172 recorded fixtures embed — the same 163 the `ModelTargetSpecSchema` widening costs
in §3.6, measured the same way and reproducing exactly. The per-batch tiering device that cuts that to
26 has nothing to key on here: `removalCombatConditional` and `removalSmallConditional` are the two
role notes describing the target-narrowing gap, and no role note anywhere describes a toll, so there
is no slot a tier could be confined to until one is written. A paid run either way, which is a
budget decision and not this document's.

### 4.2 What moved after this table was first measured

Two of the beads §6 filed have since landed, and both move numbers printed above.

**`targetRestrictionFactor` now reads `effect.target.filter`** (`mtg-xiis`, closed 2026-08-21). §3.1
reports the hole and §3.1's probe table was measured through it; the discount now reaches whichever of
the two fields narrows the target, and a target carrying both takes it once. Every score in §3.1
re-measured against today's tree: `destroy target attacking creature` moved 3.000 → **1.700**,
`destroy target attacking or blocking creature` 3.000 → **1.700**, `{R}` 3 damage to an attacking or
blocking creature 3.000 → **1.975**, `{1}{U}` return an attacking creature to hand -0.100 → **-0.600**,
`{U}` the same 0.450 → **-0.050**, `{1}{B}` -3/-3 on an attacker 1.140 → **0.770**, and `{W}` tap an
attacker 0.050 → **-0.250**. The rows in §3.1 whose targets carry a restriction did not move, which is
why rows 1 and 6 keep 1.700 as their floor and lose 3.000 as their ceiling. §5's third card scores
-0.050 today rather than the 0.450 printed there.

**The `authoredCards` cap was raised**, and the raise is already spent. §3.1 quotes
`z.array(CardSchema).max(121)` full at 121 of 121; the cap is `max(124)` today and the flagship brief
carries 124, so the bound in row 1's `Cards` column is again the binding one. Printing either
recommended family past here needs the cap raised a second time, with the census and named hole the
docblock asks for.

Neither figure in either paragraph is recomputed by any gate, which is `mtg-r9e0`'s subject. Both are
cheap to recompute: every score above is `safeParseCard` followed by
`evaluateCard(card, DEFAULT_SCORE_WEIGHTS).score` over a card literal, and every fixture count is a
walk of the two recorded corpora testing one substring per file.

## 5. The recommendation, and the first three cards

**Print conditional interaction on hand-authored instants, using `TargetFilter.combat` paired with a
`TargetRestriction`, starting with three commons in three colors.**

The pairing is deliberate. The filter alone is what makes the card interactive; the restriction
alongside it is what makes the evaluator charge for the conditionality today, before
`targetRestrictionFactor` learns to read the filter. A card printed on the pair is priced honestly
now and stays priced honestly after the fix, so the family does not have to be re-tuned when §6's
evaluator bead lands.

These are written as rules text rather than DSL, because this lane does not print cards. Scores are
the measured `evaluateCard` result for the exact shape.

**One. White common instant, `{1}{W}`.**

> Destroy target attacking or blocking creature with power 3 or greater.

Scores 1.700, against 3.000 for the unconditional `{1}{W}` "destroy target creature" the set prints
instead. This is `removalCombatConditional`, the W common instant slot, printed at last as the role
was written; and it is the direct answer to "removal is hyper-powerful", because it is removal that
misses. It cannot be cast on turn two at a mana creature and it cannot answer a blocker that never
blocks, so the opponent's decision about whether to attack becomes a decision that matters.

**Two. Red common instant, `{R}`.**

> This spell deals 3 damage to target attacking or blocking creature with power 4 or less.

Scores 1.975, against 3.000 for the unconditional `{R}` "3 damage to any target". The power ceiling
rather than a floor, because red's version of conditional removal should be the one that punishes
small aggressive boards and fails against the thing you were actually worried about. It is also the
cheapest card in the family and the one most likely to be held up, which is what makes the simulator
figure in §3.1 load-bearing: the bot already holds it and already casts it in `declareBlockers`.

**Three. Blue common instant, `{U}`.**

> Return target attacking creature to its owner's hand.

Scores 0.450. One mana, no card advantage, no permanent answer, and it only functions on the
opponent's turn — which is the shape the feedback asked for and the shape the set has none of. It
prices low and it should: it is a tempo card, and the evaluator is right that a tempo card is not a
removal spell. It is included in the first three precisely because a family made only of conditional
removal would answer half the feedback and reinforce the other half.

None of the three needs a new effect kind, a new keyword, a new target kind, a new trigger condition,
a print template, a Forge mapping, a kernel arm, a sim policy change, or a recorded fixture.

**What the recommendation does not fix.** The third complaint stands: these are still destroy, still
damage, still bounce. Conditional targeting changes _when_ a card is cast and _what question it
asks_, not which primitive it runs. The vocabulary of effects is genuinely short and the cheapest
route to lengthening it is candidate 3 above — a new trigger condition costs five declaration sites
and zero fixtures — but that is a second lane and it should follow measurement of the fire counts
rather than precede it.

## 6. Beads this lane produces

1. **`targetRestrictionFactor` never reads `effect.target.filter`.** _Filed as `mtg-xiis`, closed
   2026-08-21; §4.2 carries what moved._ `evaluate.ts:1373` applies
   `restrictedTargetFactor` when `target.restriction` is present and ignores `target.filter`
   entirely, so `destroy target attacking creature` and `destroy target creature` both score 3.000.
   A strictly weaker card prices identically to a strictly stronger one. Sibling of `mtg-gloz`, same
   file, same shape. Fixing it moves the recommended white card from 3.000 to roughly 1.700, so it
   should land before the family is tuned, or the family should be printed on the filter-plus-
   restriction shape that already prices correctly.
2. **`costReduction` is unpriced in `@mtg/deckbuild`.** The string appears nowhere in `deckbuild` or
   `sim`; a three-mana artifact scores -2.650 with and without a `{1}` creature-cost reduction.
   Second confirmed instance of the `mtg-gloz` failure mode, independent of any lever chosen here.
3. **Five `DEFAULT_TRIGGER_FIRE_COUNT` rows are guesses on the opponent-facing conditions.** The
   five `aPlayerCasts<Color>Spell` rows share one guessed 1.6, `opponentDealtNoncombatDamage` is a
   guessed 0.15, and `beginningOfEndStep` is derived rather than observed. Any card printed on those
   conditions is balanced against a number nobody measured. Blocks candidate 2 in §4.
4. **Raise `authoredCards` past 121.** _Done; the cap is 124 and the brief carries 124, so the bound
   binds again._ `brief.ts:612` caps it at 121 and it is full at 121, so the
   first card of the recommended family cannot be added without the raise, which the docblock says
   must arrive with a census and a named hole. This document is the census and the named hole is
   conditional interaction.
5. **Print the family** (the implementation lane this one recommends): the three cards above plus
   the rest of the family, hand-authored, with the balance re-sweep the fixture edit forces. Note
   that any edit to the committed flagship set fixture, of any size, moves three committed digest pins
   (`packages/metrics/test/balance/baseline.ts`, the matching precon-balance-status fixture, and
   the retained preconstructed-deck evidence) and requires the 10,035-game seeded sweep to
   re-baseline. The lever is free; shipping it is not, and that cost is the same for every candidate
   in §4 that prints a card.

## 7. Reproduction

The census, the fixture blast radius and every score in this document came from short scripts run
against this worktree. The census walks
the committed flagship set fixture under `packages/setgen/fixtures/sets/` and counts abilities by kind,
conditions by name, targets by narrowing, and `costReduction` occurrences. The blast radius walks
`packages/setgen/fixtures/llm/` and `packages/setgen/fixtures/llm-hearthglass/` and classifies each
recorded fixture by whether its answer schema embeds the effect union, a target kind or a trigger
condition, and whether its prompt prints the effect vocabulary block. The scores come from
`safeParseCard` followed by `evaluateCard(card, DEFAULT_SCORE_WEIGHTS).score` on cards constructed
inline; refusals are quoted as the validator returned them. The slot counts come from
`ROLE_PROFILES` joined against the play-booster skeleton's per-color `common.spellSlots` lists.

No paid call was made and no fixture was recorded. Nothing outside this document was modified.
